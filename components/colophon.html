Before you start

A guide for anyone who needs to understand design systems, starting from zero.

Most writing about design systems is either a sales pitch or a technical tutorial. Neither helps a founder decide whether to invest, a product manager understand what their design team is asking for, or a new designer see how all the pieces fit together. This book tries to do exactly that.

We've written it the way we'd explain things across a table: in plain language, with real situations, and with the reasoning behind every recommendation. If you've never worked with a design system, you should be able to follow every page. If you've worked with them for years, we hope you'll still find useful frameworks, honest numbers and a few new angles, especially on the day-to-day work of running a system.

It's written by Aniket Pandey, who leads Layer One, a design systems studio in Bangalore. Aniket has built design systems at IBM (for clients including Cardinal Health and Riyadh Air), led the design system at CARS24 across 10+ products in three countries, and works on design system tooling and governance at JPMorgan Chase.[[c:layerone]] The book combines that experience with the published work of practitioners such as Nathan Curtis, Brad Frost, Ben Callahan, Dan Mall and Alla Kholmatova, and with the public documentation of mature systems from IBM, Google, Atlassian, Adobe, Salesforce and the UK government.

How sure are we? Four labels

Not everything in this book is equally certain, and we think you deserve to know which is which. Claims that matter carry one of four labels:

[[std]]
A formal standard, like the accessibility guidelines (WCAG 2.2). If you need to comply, it's not optional.
[[doc]]
Published research, a case study or official documentation. Always cited, with a warning when the study is small or run by a vendor.
[[prac]]
Our recommendation from practice. Reasoned and tested, but not universal. Disagree if your situation is different.
[[ill]]
A worked example built on stated assumptions. It shows a method, not a measured result.

We don't invent statistics, case studies or results. Worked examples use Ledgerly, a fictional business finance product, so you never confuse an illustration with a real client. Every colour value, contrast ratio and type size in the figures is calculated by the book's build script, not typed by hand.
