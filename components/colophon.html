About this edition

A field guide for the people who decide whether a design system gets built, and how.

This playbook exists because most writing about design systems is either a vendor pitch or a component tutorial. Neither helps a founder decide whether to invest, a design leader plan the first year, or an engineering leader understand what they are signing up to maintain.

It is written by Aniket Pandey, who leads Layer One, a design systems studio in Bangalore. Aniket has built design systems at IBM (for clients including Cardinal Health and Riyadh Air), led the design system at CARS24 across 10+ products in three countries, and works on design system tooling and governance at JPMorgan Chase.[[c:layerone]] The playbook distils that work, plus the published practice of the most mature public systems, into decisions you can act on.

How to read the evidence

Not every claim in this book is equally certain, and we think you should know which is which. Every claim that matters carries one of four labels:

[[std]]
A normative specification, such as WCAG 2.2. If you claim conformance, it is not optional.
[[doc]]
Published research, a case study or official system documentation. Cited, with caveats where the sample is small or vendor-run.
[[prac]]
Layer One's recommendation from practice. Reasoned and tested, but not universal. Disagree when your context differs.
[[ill]]
A worked example with stated assumptions. It is not a measured result, so treat the method as the lesson, not the numbers.

We do not invent statistics, case studies or outcomes. Worked examples use Ledgerly, a fictional B2B finance product, so you never mistake an illustration for a client result. All colour values, contrast ratios and type scales in the figures are computed by the build script from the data files in the repository, not typed by hand.
