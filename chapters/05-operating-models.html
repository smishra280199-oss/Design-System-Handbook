Chapter 05

Design system operating models

A design system is not something a team builds and publishes. It is a product with users, a roadmap, releases, support and metrics. The operating model decides whether it is still trusted in three years.

Read this if

You are setting up a system team, your system has adoption problems, or product teams see the system team as a bottleneck.

What you'll leave with
  1. A clear choice between centralised, federated and hybrid models, and what each requires.
  2. Working templates: decision rights (RACI), contribution flow, versioning policy.
  3. A health scorecard and a plan for managing design and technical debt.

05.1Treat the system as a product

The most reliable predictor of a healthy design system is whether its team behaves like a product team. That means it knows its users (product designers and engineers), runs discovery on their problems, prioritises a roadmap, ships on a cadence, supports what it ships and measures outcomes.

Product practiceIn a design system team
UsersProduct designers, engineers, PMs, content designers, and sometimes vendors and partners.
DiscoveryAudit requests, sit with product teams, review what teams build outside the system and why.
RoadmapPublic, prioritised by reach (how many teams) × pain, not by what the system team finds interesting.
ReleasesVersioned, with changelogs and migration notes; on a predictable cadence.
SupportA channel with response targets, office hours, onboarding sessions.
MetricsAdoption, coverage, contribution, satisfaction and outcome KPIs (03.4).

05.2Centralised, federated and hybrid models

Nathan Curtis's 2015 essay set out the vocabulary most teams still use: solitary, centralised and federated.[[c:curtis-team-models]] Salesforce later described a cyclical hybrid, with a central team plus a federated group of contributors that inform each other.[[c:salesforce-cyclical]] Curtis himself later concluded that successful systems always have a central team and seek participation from a federated community.[[c:curtis-fallacy]] [[doc]]

ModelStrengthsFailure modeFits
SolitaryFast; grounded in real product needs.The system bends to one product; others don't adopt.Stage 1–2; a single product
CentralisedFocus, consistent quality, clear accountability.Becomes a bottleneck and drifts from product reality ("overlords don't scale").Early Stage 3, while foundations stabilise
FederatedSpreads load; strong product context.Nobody's job, so nobody does it; decisions stall.Rarely works alone
HybridCore quality plus contribution at scale.Needs real investment in contribution process and reviewer time.Stage 3–4; multiple products

[[prac]] Our recommendation

Start solitary or centralised to establish foundations and the first components. Move to hybrid as soon as two or more teams are adopting. Do it deliberately, by budgeting contributor time in product teams' plans, not by hoping for goodwill.

05.3Ownership and decision rights

Most system conflicts are really unclear decision rights. Write them down. The RACI below is a starting template for a hybrid model. Responsible does the work, Accountable makes the call, Consulted gives input, Informed is told.

Note row six. Product teams are accountable for their own local components. A healthy system lets teams build outside it when it doesn't meet their need. It just asks them to say so, so the system can learn.

05.4Design and engineering collaboration

Design–engineering friction in a system usually comes from two sources of truth disagreeing. Resolve it by deciding, explicitly, what each source is authoritative for.

Tokens

Truth for values

One token source (JSON in the repository, in the DTCG format) feeds both the design tool and code.[[c:dtcg-format]] Neither side hand-edits values.

Code

Truth for behaviour

States, keyboard interaction, responsiveness and accessibility semantics are defined by the shipped component.

Design library

Truth for intent

Usage, composition and visual specification. It mirrors code names and variants one-to-one.

One definition of done for a component

  • Designed for every state: default, hover, focus, active, disabled, loading, error, empty.
  • Built using tokens only. No raw values in component code.
  • Keyboard and screen-reader behaviour specified and tested; meets WCAG 2.2 AA.[[c:wcag22]]
  • Names, variants and properties identical in the design library and in code.
  • Documented: when to use, when not to, anatomy, examples, accessibility notes.
  • Released in a versioned package with a changelog entry.

05.5Contribution and review

Contribution is what lets a small core team serve a large organisation. It only works if the path is clear, proportional to the size of the change, and fast.

The rule of two

A component enters the core system when at least two teams need it. Until then, it lives as a local component in the team that built it, built from system tokens so it can be promoted later. This prevents the core from filling with one-off components, and it gives the system a natural pipeline of tested candidates. [[prac]]

05.6Governance and decision-making

Governance is how a system decides when people disagree. Keep it lightweight and written down:

  1. Principles settle most arguments

    "Accessible by default" and "clarity over density" end debates faster than any committee.

  2. RFCs for significant changes

    A one-page proposal (problem, options, recommendation, migration impact) open for comment for a fixed period, decided by the accountable owner.

  3. A small council for cross-org decisions

    At Stage 4, representatives of major product areas meet on a fixed cadence for decisions that affect everyone: foundations, breaking changes, roadmap.

  4. A documented exception path

    Teams may diverge with a stated reason. Exceptions are logged and reviewed quarterly; recurring exceptions become roadmap items.

Every decision comes with a reason.Rule #6 — Layer One

05.7Versioning and release management

Product teams need to upgrade on purpose, not discover changes in production. Use semantic versioning:[[c:semver]] MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH, and define what each means for a design system. Visual changes are the grey area most policies forget.

BumpMeaningDesign system examples
MAJORConsumers must change their code or will see breaking behaviour.Renamed or removed token; changed component API; removed variant; changed default size or spacing that alters layouts.
MINORNew capability, backward compatible.New component, variant, token or icon; new optional property.
PATCHFixes, no intended visual or API change.Bug fix; accessibility fix that doesn't change layout; documentation.

Release practice

  • A predictable cadence (e.g. minor releases every two weeks), with patches as needed.
  • Changelog entries written for consumers ("what changes for you"), not for the system team.
  • Design library versions published in step with code releases.
  • Visual regression tests on every component before release.

Deprecation policy

  • Deprecate in a minor release, with a console warning and docs notice.
  • Provide a migration guide, and a codemod where the change is mechanical.
  • Remove only in the next major release, after a stated minimum period.
  • Track usage of deprecated items so you know when removal is safe.

05.8Documentation and adoption

Docs decide adoption.Rule #9 — Layer One

Engineers and designers adopt what they can understand in the five minutes before a deadline. Every component page should answer, in order:

  1. What is it, and when do I use it?

    One sentence, plus when not to use it and what to use instead.

  2. What does it look like?

    Live examples of variants and states.

  3. How do I build with it?

    Copyable code; properties table; design library link.

  1. What do I need to know about accessibility?

    Keyboard behaviour, labelling requirements, known pitfalls.

  2. Do's and don'ts

    Visual examples of correct and incorrect usage.

  3. What changed?

    Version history and migration notes.

Adoption tactics that work

Pilot, then case study

Ship with one team first; publish their before-and-after internally.

Champions

One named designer and engineer per product team who get early access and a direct line.

Office hours

A weekly slot where anyone can bring a screen and get help building it with the system.

Lint rules

Flag raw colour values and deprecated components in code review, automatically.

Codemods

Automate mechanical migrations so upgrades cost minutes, not sprints.

Make the right way easiest

Starter templates and scaffolds that begin with the system already wired in.

05.10Managing technical and design debt

Every system accumulates debt: components that no longer fit, workarounds that became permanent, values that drifted. Debt is normal; invisible debt is the problem.

Debt typeHow it appearsHow to detect it
Detached instancesDesigners break components to make one-off changes.Design-tool analytics on detachments by component
Hard-coded valuesRaw colours, sizes and spacing in product code.Lint rules; scripted search
Forked componentsTeams copy a system component and modify it.Code search for copied component signatures
Deprecated usageOld components still in production after replacement.Import tracking of deprecated items
Inaccessible patternsComponents that pass in isolation but fail in composition.Automated checks on key flows; periodic manual audits
Design–code driftThe design library and code no longer match.Periodic side-by-side review; token sync checks

[[prac]] A debt budget

Keep a visible debt register, and reserve a fixed share of every cycle (we suggest around a fifth) for paying it down. Teams that schedule debt work on a fixed basis rarely need a "big rewrite"; teams that don't usually end up doing one.

Operating model checklist

  • Team model chosen and written down
  • RACI for the eight decisions in 05.3
  • Tokens, code and design library each own a defined truth
  • Definition of done for components
  • Contribution tiers and the rule of two published
  • Versioning policy including visual changes
  • Deprecation policy with minimum notice period
  • Docs template for every component
  • Health scorecard with a monthly review
  • Debt register and debt budget
